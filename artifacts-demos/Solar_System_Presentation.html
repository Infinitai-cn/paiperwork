THE SUN

Our Sun is a G-type main-sequence star at the center of our solar system, containing 99.86% of the system's total mass. This massive ball of hot plasma generates energy through nuclear fusion, converting hydrogen into helium at its core.

Surface Temperature

Approximately 5,500 degrees Celsius on the surface, while the core reaches an astounding 15 million degrees Celsius.

Diameter

About 1.4 million kilometers, making it roughly 109 times wider than Earth and large enough to fit 1.3 million Earths inside.

Age & Lifespan

Estimated at 4.6 billion years old, with approximately 5 billion years remaining in its main sequence phase.

THE INNER PLANETS

The four terrestrial planets closest to the Sun are characterized by solid, rocky surfaces composed primarily of silicate rocks and metals. These worlds represent the inner boundary of our solar system's planetary zone.

MERCURY

The smallest and fastest planet, completing an orbit in just 88 Earth days. Surface temperatures swing dramatically from -180°C to 430°C.

VENUS

Earth's "sister planet" with a thick toxic atmosphere creating the hottest surface temperature in our solar system at 465°C, hot enough to melt lead.

EARTH

The only known world harboring life, featuring vast oceans covering 71% of its surface and a protective magnetic field shielding its atmosphere.

MARS

The "Red Planet" features the solar system's largest volcano, Olympus Mons, and a canyon system, Valles Marineris, that dwarfs Earth's Grand Canyon.

THE GAS GIANTS

Jupiter and Saturn dominate the outer solar system as massive worlds composed primarily of hydrogen and helium. These giants possess powerful magnetic fields, numerous moons, and complex atmospheric dynamics that continue to fascinate astronomers.

JUPITER - King of Planets

Massive Scale

Jupiter contains more mass than all other planets combined, with a diameter 11 times larger than Earth and a mass 318 times greater.

Great Red Spot

A storm larger than Earth has raged for at least 400 years, with winds reaching 680 km/h in this iconic anticyclonic feature.

95 Known Moons

Four large Galilean moons—Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto—each possess unique characteristics worthy of scientific exploration.

SATURN - The Ringed Wonder

Spectacular Ring System

Saturn's rings span 282,000 km but are only 10 meters thick, composed of billions of ice and rock particles from microscopic to house-sized.

Lowest Density

With a density lower than water, Saturn would float if placed in an enormous cosmic bathtub—its specific gravity is just 0.69.

146 Confirmed Moons

Titan, Saturn's largest moon, possesses a thick nitrogen atmosphere and liquid methane lakes, making it a prime target for astrobiological research.

THE ICE GIANTS

Uranus and Neptune represent a distinct class of planets with compositions dominated by water, ammonia, and methane ices. These mysterious distant worlds exhibit extreme conditions that challenge our understanding of planetary science.

URANUS - The Tilted World

Extreme Axial Tilt

Uranus rotates on its side with a 98-degree tilt, likely from a massive ancient collision. This unique orientation means its poles receive more sunlight than its equator during its 84-year orbit.

Coldest Atmosphere

Despite being closer to the Sun than Neptune, Uranus holds the record for coldest planetary atmosphere at -224°C, with minimal internal heat to warm its outer layers.

27 Known Moons

Named after characters from Shakespeare and Alexander Pope's works, these moons and 13 faint rings orbit the pale blue-green planet discovered in 1781.

NEPTUNE - The Distant Giant

Windiest Planet

Neptune boasts the strongest sustained winds in the solar system, reaching speeds of 2,100 km/h. Supersonic storm systems create dynamic weather patterns observed by Voyager 2.

Great Dark Spot

A massive storm system similar to Jupiter's Great Red Spot was discovered by Voyager 2 in 1989, though it has since dissipated, demonstrating Neptune's dynamic atmosphere.

Triton - A Captured Moon

Neptune's largest moon orbits retrograde, indicating it was likely captured from the Kuiper Belt. Triton features active nitrogen geysers and a thin atmosphere.

Extreme Distance

At 4.5 billion kilometers from the Sun, Neptune takes 165 Earth years to complete one orbit. Sunlight takes over 4 hours to reach this distant world.

DWARF PLANETS & SMALL BODIES

Beyond the major planets lies a diverse population of smaller worlds that provide crucial insights into solar system formation and evolution. These objects include dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets scattered throughout the system.

Dwarf Planets

Pluto

Once the ninth planet, now the archetype of dwarf planets. Features a heart-shaped nitrogen glacier called Tombaugh Regio and five known moons, including the large Charon.

Ceres

The largest object in the asteroid belt, containing nearly a third of the belt's total mass. Recent observations suggest a subsurface ocean of briny water.

Eris, Makemake & Haumea

Trans-Neptunian objects that challenged our definition of planets. Eris is slightly smaller than Pluto but more massive, while Haumea rotates so rapidly it's egg-shaped.

Asteroids & Comets

Asteroid Belt

Between Mars and Jupiter lies a ring of over 1.9 million asteroids larger than 1 km. These remnants from planetary formation offer clues to our solar system's origin.

Kuiper Belt

A vast donut-shaped region beyond Neptune containing millions of icy objects. Pluto, Eris, and other dwarf planets reside in this frozen frontier 30-55 AU from the Sun.

Oort Cloud

A theoretical spherical shell of icy bodies extending up to 100,000 AU. Long-period comets like Hale-Bopp originate from this distant reservoir at the edge of the Sun's influence.

OUR COSMIC HOME

Our solar system represents just one small corner of the Milky Way galaxy, which contains an estimated 100-400 billion stars. As we continue to explore and understand our cosmic neighborhood, we discover new worlds, unravel mysteries of planetary formation, and search for signs of life beyond Earth.
